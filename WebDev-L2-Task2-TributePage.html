Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

The Missile Man of India · People's President

Scientist, visionary, teacher, and the 11th President of India — Dr. Kalam inspired millions to dream, innovate, and serve the nation.

The Life of a Visionary

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, into a humble Tamil Muslim family. His father, a boat owner, and his mother, a homemaker, instilled in him the values of simplicity, hard work, and integrity. Despite modest means, young Kalam was an avid learner who would rise before dawn to study by the light of a kerosene lamp.

After graduating in physics from St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, Kalam moved to Madras to study aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. His dream was to become a fighter pilot, but he narrowly missed the selection — a twist of fate that steered him toward a legendary career in space science and defence technology. He joined the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and later the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), where he played a pivotal role in India's first satellite launch vehicle (SLV-III).

Dr. Kalam's most celebrated contribution came with the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, which produced the Agni and Prithvi missiles, earning him the title "Missile Man of India". His leadership, technical brilliance, and unshakable optimism transformed India's defence capabilities and cemented his legacy as one of the nation's greatest scientists.

In 2002, Dr. Kalam was elected as the 11th President of India — a role he redefined with his warmth, humility, and deep connection with young people. He often said, "My message, especially to young people, is to have courage to think differently, to invent, to travel the unexplored path…" After his presidency, he returned to teaching and writing, inspiring countless students across the country until his passing on 27 July 2015.

"Dream, dream, dream. Dreams transform into thoughts and thoughts result in action." — Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

Key Achievements & Milestones

1960s–70s

ISRO & SLV-III

Worked on India's first satellite launch vehicle (SLV-III), successfully deploying the Rohini satellite in 1980 — a landmark achievement for Indian space technology.

1980s–90s

Missile Man of India

Led the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, developing the Agni and Prithvi missiles, and strengthening India's strategic defence capabilities.

2002–2007

President of India

Served as the 11th President of India, known as the "People's President" for his approachable nature, inspiring youth, and promoting education and innovation.

2008–2015

Wings of Fire & Teaching

Authored several books including the iconic Wings of Fire, and continued to teach and mentor students at universities across India.

2015

Legacy & Bharat Ratna

India's highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna, was awarded to him in 1997. His passing in 2015 was mourned by the entire nation — a true son of India.

Enduring Legacy

Dr. Kalam's life is a testament to the power of perseverance, humility, and vision. He remains a beacon of hope for millions of young Indians who dare to dream beyond their circumstances. His message — "You have to dream before your dreams can come true" — continues to resonate in classrooms, laboratories, and hearts across the globe.

In his honour, the Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam Memorial stands in Rameswaram, and his birthday (15 October) is celebrated as World Students' Day — a fitting tribute to a man who truly believed that the future belongs to the young.

"To succeed in your mission, you must have single-minded devotion to your goal."
